Name: Spotlight Stage
Family: Spaces and journeys
Use: story
Look: A toy theatre at night: crimson velvet, a worn timber stage and a painted world of flat card cut-outs standing in layers, with an easel card brought on at the end for the call to action. The room is dark; whatever the story is about is picked out by shafts of light cutting through haze.
Motion: It plays like a show. Props and pieces are flown in from above on thin rods, actors and props are cut-outs pushed along slots in the floor with a small wobble. Scene changes are straight cuts: the set and the wash colour change on the same frame, with nothing sliding, wiping or crossing the screen (or the camera flies to the next area of the stage). The cut-out actors travel in every shot (a hop or bob every 8-10 frames, a slight tilt), swing hinged arms and heads on pins, lean back before each big action, overshoot and settle, look before they move, swap to a shocked, proud or worried pose on the big beats, and sway gently on their rods when they wait. No lip-sync.
Light: Only stage lamps, each a real spot light with a matching visible beam: two follow spots from the house that stay on the actors, two wide front washes that set the colour of the scene, a top light that drops a shaft on the centre of the action, a small special on the easel card and a row of horizon lights behind the last flat. Light every area the camera can see (the wings, the flies and the extra places too), so no shot shows a black gap. The follow spots stay on the actors through every fast pan and zoom. The wash colour changes on the same frame as each quick scene change. The first frame is fully lit, never a fade-up from dark. Front washes about 20% brighter than a classic dark stage, so the picture averages about 40% brightness with colour strength about 45-50%. What no lamp touches falls to deep indigo, not pure black (pure black on 1% of the picture at most). The dark theatre mood stays: beams through the haze and pools of light.
Materials: Thick matt card painted in flat gouache colours with a fine ink line and paper grain, bare card on the sawn edges, thin steel rods and wires, deep-pile velvet with gold braid, dark stained boards with a satin reflection.
Camera: It leaves the stalls seat, but it moves only for a reason: a short eased pan or zoom follows an actor who travels, reveals something new or lands a reaction on its word. Between moves the framing holds, still or drifting very slowly. It pushes onto the actors, pulls back to the whole stage and pans across into the wings and up to the flies. Full and waist-up shots are home base, and nothing is a smeared whip. It stays within about 25 degrees of straight-on, so the flat cut-outs never turn edge-on. Long lens, the whole stage picture sharp.
Finish: Beams are ray-marched cones with drifting haze and dust that only shows inside them; soft shadows from each layer onto the one behind, bloom on the brightest card, filmic contrast, heavy vignette, fine grain.
Good for: Stories, history, fables and step-by-step scenes. One scene per beat: each beat is a stage picture built from flat cut-out scenery and cut-out actors, and the change between beats is a straight cut. The follow spot says where to look; the set pieces and the colour of the wash say where and when we are, with no card or sign naming the scene.
Format: Vertical 9:16 (1080x1920), 30 fps, 45-60 seconds, with a narration track at 3 words a second and no captions and no labels naming the place or what's next. The numbers for pace, shots, places, camera moves, shot sizes, lamps and movement are in references/specs.md and beat anything here or in reference.js (whose slow stalls camera, 16:9 frame, darker grade and scene-name easel cards are superseded).
