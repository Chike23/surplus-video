# The story and the script

Read all of this before step 1 (the ideas) and again before step 4 (the script). You write the script from the idea
the user picked. It meets the script-side specs in `references/specs.md`, and every fact in it is sourced. A script
the user pastes instead is used word for word: only lay it out (the file format below) and check it.

The job: a stranger mid-scroll stops in the first second, can't leave before the end, and walks away with something
they'll repeat to a friend. Surplus is the last 10–15 % and has to feel earned, not stapled on.

## The two kinds (the user picks one first)
**Twist: a viral story with a twist.** A true story most people have never heard, from any era, with a physical cause
and effect you can show on a stage, and a last beat that flips what we thought was going on. It is a story first: it
would be worth watching with no app at the end. Its turn is about eating, food, appetite, strength or the body, so the
bridge to gaining weight is one short line.

**Relatable: an informative story the viewer lives.** A moment the viewer has had ("you order the big meal and tap out
at half"), the surprising fact or mechanism behind it, and one character living it until the turn. The viewer
recognises themselves in the first three seconds and leaves knowing something they didn't. Surplus is how the
character fixes it.

Both kinds turn on **one surprising story, fact or experience.** Never a tip list, never a generic "how to gain weight"
explainer, never a story that's only there to get to the app.

## What makes an idea right for this format
- One surprising story with a physical cause and effect you can show: something happens to an object or a body, on
  stage, that the viewer can see.
- A clear everyman character (a baker, a soldier, a student, a ship's cook, a lighthouse keeper), stakes that escalate,
  and a twist in the last story beat.
- A setting built from simple 3D shapes plus cut-out cards: towns, bridges, ships, castles, deserts, labs, palaces,
  kitchens, gyms, school halls.
- **Twist:** mix the 10 across eras and themes: ancient, medieval, 1800s, modern, science, animals, inventions,
  disasters, weird laws, lucky accidents.
- **Relatable:** mix the 10 across everyday moments: school or work lunch, family dinner, eating out, the gym, sport,
  mornings, a date, holidays, travel, being sick, a growth spurt.
- **True, checked:** verify the key facts with a web search before offering the idea. Don't present a viral legend as
  fact. If the user wants a legend anyway, frame it as "legend says" in the script.
- **Rules:** stories, characters and shots are always original. Never recreate another creator's video shot for shot;
  if the user brings one, offer an original take on the same idea. No real, named living people as characters, and
  no real living person as the butt of a joke. No brand mascots or logos. Nothing graphic, nothing political.
- Not too similar to a done story (`references/stories-done.md`), and not too similar to another of the 10.

In chat, show only the number, title, era, hook line and twist (one or two lines each). The doc has everything.

## The hook: the first three seconds decide everything
The first line is the strangest true thing in the story, said as a fact, in about 10 words or fewer, and the first
frame already shows it happening. It opens one question the viewer needs answered, and only the end answers it.
- **Start in the middle**, at the moment it's already strange. No set-up, no name first ("Meet Sam", "This is Sam"),
  no "Did you know", "Have you ever", "Here's why", "In 1854,", no question, no title card.
- **A picture, not a concept.** "The ship's cook ate double rations and stayed the thinnest man aboard." not
  "Sailors had surprising diets."
- **A contradiction or a stake:** two things that shouldn't go together (strongest and chubby; eats all day and can't
  gain; a law about bread), or something about to be lost.
- **A number only if it shocks and can be pictured.**
- **Relatable hooks name the viewer's own moment and flip it:** "You eat until you're stuffed. That's exactly why the
  scale won't move."
- **Write ten hooks for the picked idea, keep the best,** and test it: would a stranger mid-scroll stop? Can the first
  frame show it? Does it promise something only the end delivers? Is it true? Any "no" means rewrite.

Hook shapes (adapt; never copy anyone's line; the facts in these are placeholders, check your own):
- **Impossible fact:** "<Ordinary thing> once <extraordinary consequence>."
- **Contradiction:** "The strongest men in <place> were fed to be fat."
- **Stakes in progress:** "Tom has thirty days to gain six kilos, or he's off the team."
- **Consequence first:** "One missing breakfast cost <someone> <something big>."
- **The viewer's habit, flipped:** "You skip breakfast because you're not hungry. Your body planned that."

## The story engine: keep them watching every second
- **Want, obstacle, stakes by second eight.** We know who, what they want, and what they lose if they fail.
- **"But" and "so", never "and then".** Every beat is caused by the last one and turns it (a setback or a discovery).
  Two beats joined by "and then": cut one or merge them.
- **Escalate.** Each beat raises the stakes or the strangeness. The biggest beat comes right before the twist.
- **Open loops.** The hook opens the big question. Every 8–10 seconds open a smaller one ("but the doctor had a
  reason", "that's when the scale did something odd") and close it within the next beat. The viewer always has
  something pending.
- **Plant, then pay off.** The twist is planted early: an object, a line, a detail we saw and didn't notice. A twist
  from nowhere feels cheap; a planted one feels clever and gets rewatched.
- **The twist flips the meaning** of what came before, not just a fun fact tacked on. Test: does the hook read
  differently after the twist?
- **Specific beats general:** a name, a place, a number, an object. One clear image per beat.
- **Feeling we can see:** hope, embarrassment, triumph, shown by the puppet (pose swaps, a glance, a slump), never
  named by the narrator.
- **Cut the slack.** A line nobody would miss goes.

## Show, don't tell
The picture tells the story. The narration says only what the picture can't.
- Never name a feeling ("Sam was embarrassed"). The puppet shows it (a slumped pose swap, a look at the full plate);
  the narration gives the fact that caused it.
- Never describe what's on screen ("here you can see the scale go up"). The needle swings on the word.
- The narration carries what pictures can't: time ("three weeks later"), cause ("because his stomach..."), the
  number, the twist in words.
- **Every line has a picture.** In script.md each line gets a *We see* line under it. If a line has no picture,
  rewrite it until it has one, or cut it.
- **The picture lands first:** the reaction or reveal happens 0–3 frames before its word, so the eye leads and the
  ear confirms.
- Verbs and objects over adjectives: "He ate the whole loaf" beats "He ate a lot".
- Labels and lettering name things (a sign, a sack, a number on a dial). They never repeat the narration as a caption.

## The shapes (about 55 s, about 160 words)
**Twist**
| Part | When | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 s | The strangest true moment, already happening. Opens the big question. |
| "You see," | 3–12 s | Who they are, what they want, what's at stake. A small loop opens. |
| Escalation | 12–38 s | 2–3 beats, each in a new place, each a "but" or a "so", the stakes rising, one concrete number or image each. Plant the twist. |
| Twist | about 38–47 s | The flip, planted earlier. The hook now means something new. |
| Payoff | last 10–15 % | Bridge to today: the same puppet in a quick on-stage costume swap (or a modern twin with the same problem). The narration says what Surplus does for them, they act out the change, the CTA ends it. |

**Relatable**
| Part | When | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 s | A moment the viewer has lived, and the surprising truth about it, as fact. They think "that's me". |
| "You see," | 3–12 s | The real cause in plain words, with one number. A loop opens ("but it gets stranger"). |
| Proof | 12–36 s | The character lives it in 2–3 places. Each beat shows the cause at work and gets worse, up to the low point. |
| The turn | about 36–47 s | The counter-intuitive fix, or what was really going on all along. |
| Payoff | last 10–15 % | Surplus is how the character does the fix (features from surplus.md). They act out the change; the CTA ends it. |

## The numbers (references/specs.md)
- Mark on v4 runs at about 3 words a second at his own pace, so **45–60 s is about 130–195 words**. Aim for
  150–170 (about 50–57 s).
- **The hook:** a surprising action, stated as fact, inside the first 4 s (the first ~12 words), on screen from the
  first frame. Aim for 3 s.
- **"You see,"** starts the line right after the hook and explains it, or it brings the reveal about two-thirds in.
- **A new place every ~20 words** (6–7 s): each beat moves somewhere new on the stage.
- **The payoff lands in the last 10–15 %** (from about word 85 % on) and the script ends on it. It is the character's
  result with Surplus, and its last line is the CTA. No outro, no sign-off after it.

## Surplus, through the character
Never write a line that needs the app on screen ("tap here", "look at this screen", "open the app and..."): the picture
never shows a phone or the app. The narration says what Surplus does **for the character**, and the picture shows the
character doing it: eating the extra meal, the scale's needle finally moving, a proud pose. For example: "So Sam
stopped guessing. Surplus counted every bite and set him five hundred calories over. Six weeks later, the needle
moved. Search Surplus on the App Store." Features only from `references/surplus.md`. In a Twist story the bridge is one
line that ties the twist to the viewer ("They had a whole empire feeding them. You've got Surplus.").

## Writing for the ear
- Short clauses (≤ 14 words between bridges), one idea each, everyday words. The character by name, the viewer as
  "you".
- Concrete beats vague: "a handful of almonds is 160 calories" beats "nuts are calorie-dense".
- Numbers as they should be said: "five hundred calories", "half a kilo".
- **No pauses: write it to flow.** The voice is never cut or re-timed, and every full stop makes the narrator stop
  for about a second, a comma for about half. So join the beats with bridges ("and", "but", "so", "because",
  "which is", "and get this") and keep full stops for the payoff and the CTA at the end. Line breaks in script.md
  don't make pauses; punctuation does.
- **Expressive, not flat:** one long run-on reads monotone. Give it one question, one exclamation and a dash before
  the reveal, so the voice rises and falls. The take the owner picked, as the model:
  "...hungry again by three, so he tries one trick, and get this — it's just a handful of nuts between every meal,
  which is six hundred extra calories and he never even feels stuffed! Surplus counted every bite and set his target
  five hundred over, and six weeks later? The needle finally moved. Search Surplus on the App Store."
- **The script sets the pace.** v4 ignores the speed setting, so too slow means more bridges and fewer commas, and
  too fast means a full stop at a turn. Short, plain words help too.
- Tone from `references/surplus.md`: a coach who has been skinny too; no hype words, no body-shaming.

## The file: P/script.md
```
# <title>
Kind: Twist | Relatable   CTA: link-in-bio | app-store | comment KEYWORD

## Narration
1. [S1] The ship's cook ate double rations and stayed the thinnest man aboard.
   - We see: a beanpole cut-out cook, mid-stride on a rolling deck, as a second bowl flies in on a rod and he wolfs it.
2. [S1] You see, ...
   - We see: ...
...
## Sources
- <every fact or number above, with where it comes from>
```
- One numbered line = one sentence or phrase. `[S1]`–`[S4]` at the start of a line opens that story part (3–4 parts).
  Lines without a tag stay in the current part. The indented *We see* lines are not spoken (voice.mjs reads only the
  numbered lines).

## Check before voicing
- **The hook:** the strangest true thing, as fact, within the first ~10 words; no set-up, no question; the first frame
  can show it; it opens a question only the end answers.
- **The engine:** want, obstacle and stakes by second eight; every beat a "but" or a "so"; stakes rise; a loop open at
  all times; the twist or turn planted earlier and changing what the hook means.
- **Show, don't tell:** every line has a *We see*; no named feelings; no line describing what's on screen.
- 130–195 words (150–170 aimed); written to flow (full stops only at the end, one question, one exclamation, a dash
  before the reveal); "You see," right after the hook or about two-thirds in; a new place every ~20 words;
  3–4 story parts; no `[pause]` marks.
- The payoff starts after about 85 % of the words, ends the script, and its last line is the picked CTA.
- Surplus told through the character; no line that needs a phone or the app on screen; claims only from surplus.md.
- Every number and health claim has a source (web search) and stays inside the health guard rails in surplus.md.
  A legend is said as "legend says".
- For a script the user pasted: their words exactly (only line breaks, `[S#]` and *We see* lines added); a claim that
  fails goes back to them; script-side specs it misses are listed at step 9. Never fix their words yourself.
