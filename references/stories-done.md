## Too similar = don't suggest it
A new topic or story is too similar to a done one when **any one** of these matches:
1. **The core fact or number** it turns on (e.g. "people misjudge how much they eat", "300–500 kcal a day is the right
   pace", "the stomach adapts to how much you eat").
2. **The hook or the myth it flips** (e.g. "you eat all day but can't gain", "you're gaining at the wrong speed").
3. **The lesson the viewer leaves with** (e.g. "track everything for three days", "eat a little more each meal").
4. **The character's situation and turn** (e.g. a skinny guy who thinks he eats a lot finds out he doesn't). Use a new
   character each time unless the user asks for a recurring one.

A shared broad subject (calories, appetite, the scale) is fine when all four differ. The Surplus payoff (it tracks
calories and sets a daily surplus target) is in every video and doesn't count. When in doubt, it's too similar: pick
another.

### Stretch: how competitive eaters train their stomachs
`~/shorts/stomach-training` (made with the make-short skill) · cut-out style
- **Core fact:** the stomach adapts to how much you usually eat. A speed eater's stomach became a giant sac on X-ray
  (36 hot dogs vs. 7); dieters' stomachs shrank about 27 % in four weeks. Forced stretching is dangerous.
- **Hook:** competitive eaters swallow 20,000+ calories in ten minutes.
- **Lesson:** the slow, safe version: a little more each meal, snacks between meals, no filling up on drinks first,
  calorie-dense food (NHS advice).
- **Character:** Theo, a skinny young man who can't finish one plate, next to a speed eater and a doctor.
- **Payoff:** Theo ends up asking for seconds; plug: download Surplus to start your gain.
